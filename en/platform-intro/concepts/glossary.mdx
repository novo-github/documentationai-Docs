## Find the Label You See on Screen

Each entry keeps English and German interface labels together. Search for the exact caption, such as **PRO**, **AA**, **AG**, **WDB**, or **Vorber.** PRO means Performance; AA is Work Scrap; AG is the operation-priority chip; WDB is the Knowledge Base. Definitions link to the page where you can use the concept.

Related references: [calculations](/en/platform-intro/concepts/calculations) and [roles and access](/en/platform-intro/concepts/roles-and-access).

## A

<a id="aa" />

### AA / Work / Arbeits-Ausschuss

Work Scrap: rejected parts caused by processing rather than the material. Kept separate from MA material scrap.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/data-acquisition).

### Accessibility patterns

Optional dots for Idle and diagonal lines for Off, making states distinguishable without color alone.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/machines).

### Active Sessions

Devices signed in to your account. Individual sessions can be signed out remotely.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/user-profile).

### Active shift

An **Active shift** starts at idle, production, or preparation and ends according to the machine’s duration settings. The assigned shift group is not used. Switching on removes scheduled shifts from today onward but keeps shifts already recorded today. Switching off ends the running active shift immediately and creates scheduled shifts from the group from today.

**Waiting for the next active shift** and **waiting for machine start** mean no new active shift has started yet. Planned shifts from the assigned group appear until the machine begins idle, production, or preparation. The machine still uses active shifts.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-configuration).

An operation is one production step. The dashed OP chip (AG in German) shows operation priority; P shows order priority.

### Assignment / Zuweisung

One operation placed on a machine or in a hall pool; intervals record the actual work on it.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/assignment-planner).

### Forecaster

Shows expected start and finish times from current work, waiting jobs, and planned shifts. It updates by itself about every minute and never changes your plan or delivery dates by itself. Open **How does the Forecaster work?** for its explanation.

#### Expected and Planned Times

Expected times show when work is likely to happen, including delays. Planned times show the schedule you set. The **delivery date** is the customer promise; the **planned end** is the finish in your plan.

#### At Risk

**at risk** (previously **tight**) is an amber warning. The order is not late yet, but the remaining work barely fits or an earlier step is not planned. The help calls this **At risk**. Only the name changed; the warning means the same thing.

#### On Time, Late, and Finish Not Known

- **On time**: green; expected to finish before the date.
- **Late +3 h**: red; expected to finish three hours after the date. **Late, date passed** means the date has passed and the order is unfinished.
- **Finish not known**: blue; the date is ahead, but a step has no machine or no date, so no finish can be worked out.

Missing the delivery date is red. Missing only the planned end is amber.

#### Times May Be Too Early

**Times may be too early** (previously **Forecast not reliable**) warns that an order may finish later than shown. Check for steps without a machine or date. Missing planned durations, missing shift plans, or delayed production entries can also make expected times too early.

#### Steps on a Machine

The step count tells you how many of the order's steps have a machine. It does not count finished steps. Work without a machine can make the expected finish too early.
#### Partly Planned and Not Planned at All

**Partly planned** means some steps still lack an assignment. **Not planned at all** identifies orders with a delivery date but no operation assigned to a machine or hall pool. They appear on no planning board.

#### Earlier Step Not Planned

**Earlier step not planned** warns that the times shown assume that step is already finished. Check for a missing machine or date, a hall-pool step, work never started, or a hold beyond its limit before relying on later finishes.

#### Never Started and No Planned Start

**Never started** identifies work planned more than three days ago that no longer holds up the forecast queue. **No planned start** means no forecast can be made for that assignment. Neither means the work is complete.

#### On Hold Too Long

**On hold too long** means paused work has passed **Hold limit (working hours)**. It stops holding the machine in the forecast, and assignments behind it move up. Administrators set the limit using machine working time.

#### Load and Earliest Availability

**Load** in Assignment Gantt compares booked working time with visible shift time. The machine summary shows work still booked by the forecast. When work has no expected end, the machine’s displayed free time is only its earliest possible availability.

#### Save as Plan

**Save as plan** changes eligible queue positions and planned dates in Watchmen after review. Original dates remain available beside them. A preview does not save a plan, and delivery dates stay unchanged.

#### Jobs in Parallel

**Jobs in parallel** is how many assignments a machine can run at the same time. Administrators set a whole number from 1 to 100 in machine configuration; the forecast uses this capacity.

### Hall Pool

Shared hall queue. An assignment has no specific machine until a machine in that hall claims it from PDA. In Assignment Planner, **Hall pools** lets you choose the halls and their placement. **In the board** puts pools beside their machines; **Beside the list** keeps them beside the unassigned list while machines scroll. Pools do not count toward the 25-machine limit. **Collapse pool** leaves a narrow strip that still accepts cards; **Expand pool** shows its cards again.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/assignment-planner).

### PDA Assist

**PDA Assist** (previously **PDA Assistant**) combines interval assistance and warnings for a machine. Administrators configure them separately. Operators can choose suggested times and review, snooze, or mute warnings.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-configuration).

#### Right now

**Right now** in the **PDA Assist** dialog shows current assistance information. Open it at the right edge of the terminal. An amber dot means automatic ending is counting down; green means no countdown. A brief glow follows a corrected time.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/data-acquisition).

#### Setup (preparation)

**Setup (preparation)** groups the allowed setup times separately from production in **PDA Assist**. Administrators can change the two groups independently. These options also guide reviews of past intervals.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-configuration).

#### Other times

**Other times** lists earlier alternatives to the most recent main choices, newest first. They are real machine events within the asking limit, with up to 20 offered times in total. Times outside the automatic limit require your choice.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/data-acquisition).


### Adjusted / Auto-ended / Improved

**Adjusted** marks a start or end changed by interval assistance. **Auto-ended** marks a production interval ended after the configured off or idle period; quantities may still need booking. **Improved** marks a change applied when reviewing past intervals. Open the explanation for the time and reason.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/data-acquisition).

### Improve intervals

A check of recorded intervals against machine activity for up to 60 days. It proposes changes and shows their impact before you apply them. Anyone with machine access can review one machine; only administrators can review several and apply the best options everywhere.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/workflows/improve-intervals).

### Overlaps (warning)

More intervals ran at once than the machine's parallel capacity. Parallel work may be correct. These suggestions start unticked and are excluded from **Apply best everywhere**.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-details).

### Peak Optimizer

Admin-only comparison of operators over a period to find top performers and bottlenecks.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/peak-optimizer).

### Pinned items

Pages, machines, and hall plans pinned to the top of personal navigation.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/machines).

### Planned Start / End / Actual Start / End

Planned dates describe the schedule; actual dates describe recorded work. Grouped order dates span the order; assignment rows have their own windows.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/orders-products).

### Polling interval

How often an Automator rule is checked.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/automator).

### Prep / Prep. / Vorber. / Rüst / Preparation / Vorbereitung / Rüsten

Setup before production. Vorber. is used on German PDA buttons; Rüst appears on planner cards. Both describe preparation.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/data-acquisition).

### Prep / Start / Stop / Continue

Preparation, production start, interval end, and resume controls. Explicit actions also include Start/End Preparation and Start/End Shift.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/data-acquisition).

### Position in the queue

**Position in the queue** shows where a waiting step stands in **Up next**. Running steps have no number.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-details).

### Priority

Urgency number, higher meaning more urgent. Company levels can be customized; the order form and custom settings expose different ranges.

### Products more / less produced than detected

### P\{code\} / P0–P9 / P1–P9

## R

### Ratio thresholds

Read the selected condition: setup and cycle checks compare time with a plan or baseline; production checks measure a shortfall against pace. Order progress compares completion with the elapsed schedule. The same percentage does not mean the same thing for every measure.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/automator).

### Report archive

**Report archive** contains **Weekly**, **Monthly**, and **Export** reports. **Scheduled** means a scheduled report; **By user** means someone requested it.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Queued** | Waiting for creation to start. |
| **Building** | Being created. |
| **Ready** | Ready to download. |
| **Failed** | Creation did not finish successfully. |
| **Refreshing** | Being checked against current data. |

**Rebuild with current data** checks an existing report and updates it if the contents changed. **Not emailed** describes email delivery, not creation status. A failed refresh keeps the previous version available.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/report-archive).

### Roadmap

## W

### Waiting on a previous step / Blocked by

⚠️ **Waiting on a previous step** means a step with a lower number in the same work order is neither completed nor cancelled. An earlier step without an assignment also counts as unfinished. **Start anyway** remains available; review the dependency before using it.

In Data Acquisition V3, the **Waiting on a previous step** sorting rule offers **At the bottom** or **At the top**. It comes before priority and date. If you also add **Running and on-hold orders**, that rule comes first. With **Sort by Queue Position** on, **Orders waiting on a previous step** offers **Keep in place** or **At the bottom**, preserving queue order within each group. Both options are off until you choose them and are saved for your account.

[Learn more](/en/platform-intro/pages/data-acquisition).
