## Overview

**Access:** Any role with access to this machine. [Roles and access](/en/platform-intro/concepts/roles-and-access).

Support live shop-floor execution and event recording for a single machine.

Users usually open it from the Machine Details page.

To switch machines, select the machine name in the top navigation bar. The dropdown groups the other machines you can access by hall and opens the selected machine's Data Acquisition screen directly.

## Access

<Callout kind="info" title="Access details">
- Visibility: Protected
- Primary routes: `/machines/:machineId/dataAcquisition`
</Callout>

## What You Can See

- Assignment lists and live order context in open-assignment or time-window views.
- Personalized assignment columns, ordering, and sorting.
- Start, preparation, and stop controls.
- Interval history, notes, tickets, and knowledge context.

## What You Can Do

- Start or stop work.
- Record produced quantity, material scrap, and work scrap when stopping or continuing an interval.
- Create or use live orders.
- Search for unplanned operations and assign them directly to the machine.
- Configure the information and ordering shown on assignment cards.
- Apply timeline reasons and review related support content.

## Choosing and Understanding Work

Use open-assignment or time-window views to find the work for the current machine. If an operation has not been planned, you can search for it and assign it directly from this page. Operation sequence numbers on job chips help identify the order of work, and operation and assignment notes provide instructions and context.

In Data Acquisition V3, **Sort by Queue Position** displays the order set by dragging assignments. Turn it off to display work by rules such as priority or date. For these rules, choose ascending or descending order. The rules form a numbered list: rule 1 decides first, and later rules break ties. To change their precedence, change the selection in a line’s list, remove a rule with the red remove button, or add a line with **Add sort rule**. These general sorting choices are also available in the [Assignment Planner](/en/platform-intro/pages/assignment-planner).

### Choose Where Running and On-Hold Orders Appear

**Running and on-hold orders**: Orders in progress or on hold. Example: an order that was started and is on hold for a tool change. This differs from ⚠️ **Waiting on a previous step**, where an earlier step is unfinished.

Open **Card Display Settings → Sort Order** using the gear icon. Your sorting mode determines the choices:

- With **Sort by Queue Position** on, **Running and on-hold orders** appears above the waiting-order choice. Previously, these orders stayed on top automatically. They now keep their place in the queue by default with **Keep in place**. Choose **Running and on-hold orders → At the top** to bring them above other orders. In Assignment Planner, running and on-hold cards still stay on top.
- With **Sort by Queue Position** off, use **Add sort rule** and select **Running and on-hold orders**. Choose **At the top** or **At the bottom**. This rule stays first. The waiting-order rule comes next if you add it, followed by priority and date rules.

Select **Save** to apply the display choices to the machine's order list and **Pool**. These choices are separate from the company sorting default. Follow the [display setup steps](/en/platform-intro/workflows/data-acquisition#configure-the-data-acquisition-v3-display).

### Put Waiting Orders After Work That Can Start

Orders marked ⚠️ **Waiting on a previous step** have an earlier step in the same work order that is neither completed nor cancelled. Step numbers determine the sequence. An earlier step without an assignment also counts as unfinished. For example, step #70 waits until #60 is finished.

Use the gear icon in the top bar to open **Card Display Settings → Sort Order**. Choose the option for your sorting mode:

- With **Sort by Queue Position** off, click **Add sort rule**. A new numbered rule line appears. Select **Waiting on a previous step** in that line’s list. Instead of the up/down arrow, this rule has a button that starts at **At the bottom**, placing waiting orders after other work. Click it to switch to **At the top** and review waiting orders first. This rule comes before priority and date, but after **Running and on-hold orders** when that rule is present. Your remaining rules sort work within each group.
- With **Sort by Queue Position** on, use **Orders waiting on a previous step**. **Keep in place** keeps the usual queue display. **At the bottom** places waiting work after other orders. Use the separate **Running and on-hold orders** choice to bring active work to the top. The queue order set in Assignment Planner stays the same within each group.

Select **Save** to apply your choice. Neither option moves waiting work until you choose it. Your choice is saved for your account and applies to the machine's order list and the **Pool** tab. These waiting-order options are only available in Data Acquisition V3, not in Assignment Planner.

Once no earlier step remains unfinished, the next refresh places the order in the group that can start. Follow [Configure the Data Acquisition V3 Display](/en/platform-intro/workflows/data-acquisition#configure-the-data-acquisition-v3-display).

### Use the Company Sorting Default

If you have no personal sorting saved for Data Acquisition V3, it starts with the company's sort mode and shared rules. Administrators set these in [Assignment Planner](/en/platform-intro/pages/assignment-planner). Saving unrelated display changes keeps you following that default.

When a company default exists, the reset control is labelled **Reset to company sorting**. Open **Card Display Settings → Sort Order → Reset to company sorting** to copy the current company choices. When your choices already match, the button shows a tick and is unavailable. Otherwise, it resets the choices in the open settings; select **Save** to apply them. With no company default, the icon labelled **Reset to default (no company default set yet)** restores the standard choices.

Resetting keeps the queue-mode choices for running and waiting orders. It replaces the sort rules, so add any running- or waiting-order rules you need again. These special rules are not part of the company default. Once you save your own sorting or a reset, later company changes do not replace it.

## Interval Times and Timeline Display

An interval records a stretch of preparation or production. In Data Acquisition V3, open the order's interval list. Tap a start or end time once to open **Edit interval** on **Start** or **End**. The clock icon and **Tap to change** hint identify editable times. The pencil opens **Quantities**.

Times show hours and minutes. A time from another day also shows the date as day/month, for example 20/10 14:30. A running end reads **Still running**.

### Review and Change an Interval

The summary above the controls shows the order, interval number, saved type, times, and quantities. **Start** sits on the left and **End** on the right. The other time stays visible; tap its card to switch to it. Your edits stay when you switch tabs. Dots mark unsaved changes. When both times exist, changing either time shows the new duration beside **New length:**.

- On a touch screen, turn the day, hour, and minute wheels.
- On a computer, use **−** and **+**, hold a button to repeat, or type hours and minutes. You can also use the mouse wheel. Minute and hour steps carry into the next hour or day.
- **−5 min** and **+5 min** move the selected time by five minutes. Repeated taps add up.
- Under **Quantities**, correct **Good qty.**, **Material Scrap**, and **Work Scrap**. Type quantities with a keyboard on a computer or use the number pad on a touch screen. In cumulative mode, enter the full machine counter reading.
- Choose **Production** or **Preparation** to correct the interval type. The summary shows the proposed type beside the saved one.

On screens with little height, the summary at the top takes two lines and the number pad is smaller. On very short screens, such as 1024×600 terminals, the wheels show fewer rows with **−5 min** and **+5 min** beside them, and the hint below the controls is hidden.

Select **Save** to apply your edits together. **Interval saved.** confirms success; the interval table and timeline update. **Cancel** discards edits before saving. If you already selected **Save**, cancelling does not undo a save that finishes afterwards; check the table.

### Limits When Editing

You can move the date back as far as seven days before the saved time. You cannot choose a time later than now.

When you change a time, the end must be later than the start, and neither time may be in the future. **Save** stays unavailable if nothing changed, a time error remains, or saving is still in progress. If saving fails, the dialog stays open with a message so you can try again.

For a running interval, **End** explains: **This interval is still running. Its end is set when it is stopped.** Use the normal stop action to end it. You cannot clear a saved end. A running interval cannot change to a type that already has another running interval on the same order; the unavailable option has an explanation.

Tapping outside does not close the dialog while you have unsaved changes. Follow [Correct an Interval](/en/platform-intro/workflows/data-acquisition#correct-an-interval).

### Timeline Display

Data Acquisition V3 settings offer two separate choices:

- **Timeline display → Show intervals in graph** controls whether interval bars appear on the graph.
- **Auto-refresh interval** controls how often assignments and hall pools refresh automatically.

Timeline entries for idle and stopped periods update as you switch between running, paused, and stopped states. Review these entries and their reasons to keep a clear record of interruptions. See [Run Data Acquisition](/en/platform-intro/workflows/data-acquisition) for the correction and display procedures.

## Production Quantities, Scrap, and Completion

When stopping or continuing an interval, you can enter produced quantity and record **material scrap** and **work scrap** separately. Keeping the two scrap categories separate lets you review the breakdown and their combined total in [Orders & Products](/en/platform-intro/pages/orders-products) and [Order Analysis](/en/platform-intro/pages/order-detail). Displayed production totals include the submitted scrap quantities.

Starting or stopping production updates the related order status. An assignment is marked complete when the produced quantity exactly matches the planned quantity. An over-production prompt can help you review completion when production exceeds the plan.

## PDA / BDE Buttons and Quantity Labels

**PDA (Production Data Acquisition)** and **BDE (Betriebsdatenerfassung)** are the same operator screen. Tabs include **Assignments**, **Pool**, **Downtime reasons**, **Tickets**, and **Knowledge Base**.

**Prep** starts preparation (German **Vorber.**; also called Rüsten), **Start** starts production, **Stop** ends an interval, and **Continue** resumes. Explicit actions include Start/End Preparation and Start/End Shift.

**Good qty. / Gutmenge** is good output. **Mat. / MA** is Material Scrap; **Work / AA** is Work Scrap. **Total = Input + MA + AA**. **Rework Qty** is the amount requiring rework. **Cycle Count** is sensor-detected; **PDA Entry / BDE-Eintrag** is operator-entered. Switching the progress source changes only the display.

**Example:** 95 entered good units, 3 material scrap, and 2 work scrap produce a total of 100. In **Cumulative** mode, enter the full machine counter, for example 1450, rather than this interval's increment.

## Mid Booking, Hall Pools, and Dependencies

**Mid Booking (Zwischenbuchung)** saves quantities while the job continues by closing and restarting the interval. If restart fails, quantities stay saved; press **Start** to resume. Check the running state before booking again.

In the **Pool** tab, claim work from the machine's hall pool. If no assignment exists, search unplanned operations or use **Find/Create Immediate Order**. **Job Context** combines order, work-order, operation, and assignment notes.

**Waiting on a previous step / Blocked by** means an earlier operation is unfinished. **Start anyway** remains available; review the dependency before proceeding. The concurrent-order prompt asks for confirmation if another assignment is running. Over-production offers **Put on hold** or **Mark as finished**.

Unbound or unlabeled machine time belongs to no order. Deleting a recorded interval is irreversible.

## Choose the Assignment View

| Mode | Included work |
| --- | --- |
| **All Open** | All open assignments, with no date filter. |
| **Time Window + Open** | Open assignments in a ±1/3/5/7-day window; useful for daily work. |
| **Time Window + All Statuses** | Includes completed and cancelled work for looking back. |

**Card Display Settings / Columns / Highlight** are saved per user. **Sort by Queue Position** follows the planned queue. Choose where running and waiting orders appear under **Sort Order**.

## Check Assisted Interval Times

When an administrator enables **PDA Assist**, late starts and stops can move to times shown by the machine data. It applies to production and preparation on Data Acquisition V3. Manual time edits and mid-bookings do not receive these automatic adjustments.

The choice dialog compares **Now** with **After** and shows which order gains or loses time. Choose a main option or expand **Other times** for earlier choices, newest first. You can also tap a marker on the timeline. Keep the time you pressed if none fits. Closing the initial choice dialog keeps that time.

After an automatic adjustment, **Undo** restores the time you pressed. **Change…** opens the alternatives; **Back to the time you pressed** restores the original time there. One suitable time within the automatic limit can apply immediately. With several times, your machine's settings decide whether to ask or choose automatically. Times beyond the automatic limit always need your choice.

The interval list marks changes as **Adjusted**, **Auto-ended**, or **Improved**. Open the explanation to see the changed time and reason. **Auto-ended** means a forgotten production interval ended after the configured off or idle period. It works even when the terminal is closed. Check quantities afterwards: automatic ending does not book them. Preparation never ends this way.

When automatic ending is enabled, **Machine off, order still running** can show the remaining minutes. Snoozing or muting this warning does not stop automatic ending. Ask an administrator to change it in [Machine Configuration](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-configuration).

Interval changes appear immediately on the timeline. The update after a change keeps your selected shift if it still exists. Regular and manual refreshes return to the live shift. To review past days, use **Improve intervals** on [Machine Details](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-details), following [Review and Improve Intervals](/en/platform-intro/workflows/improve-intervals).

### See What Assistance Is Active

Tap **PDA Assist** at the right edge of Data Acquisition V3. It appears when at least one interval-assistance option or warning is enabled. On a phone it sits beside the action bar, above any warning banner.

The dialog lists what assistance does on this machine. **Right now** shows current information, such as the remaining time before automatic ending or the last corrected start or end. The dot is amber only during the automatic-ending countdown; otherwise it is green. It briefly glows after assistance changes a time. The countdown says the order will end unless the machine restarts. To prevent automatic ending, ask an administrator to change the [machine settings](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-configuration).

### Understand the Offered Times

Only times shown by machine activity or the previous interval's end are offered. The main choices use the most recent activity. Earlier choices appear under **Other times**, newest first, with up to 20 offered times in total. Times before the previous order's end or beyond the machine's asking limit are left out, never moved to a limit.

Open the information icon beside the choice dialog's title for a short explanation. Missing data shorter than 10 minutes and idle, off, or preparation interruptions shorter than 2 minutes do not split an activity stretch. For example, a five-minute data gap during production does not turn the return of data into a new production start.

## Choose a Downtime Reason

**Downtime reasons** offers only the reasons available on this machine. A tile can show a code in its corner. Use the category chips above the tiles to narrow the list; these appear only when reasons have categories.

Saved tile arrangements keep newly available reasons and drop deleted ones. Other timeline reason pickers show reasons alphabetically with the code before the name. **Remove label** clears the selected reason without deleting the downtime period.

If a reason is no longer allowed, the list updates. Choose an available reason or ask an administrator to check the machine's selection. A historical reason may still appear even when it is no longer selectable. See [Downtime Reasons FAQs](/en/help-center/faq/downtime-reasons).

## PDA Assist Warnings

**PDA Assist** shows warnings for this machine when an administrator enables them. Only administrators can change warning settings. It is separate from classic Alert Rules and Automator.

| Warning | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Producing without an order** | The machine is producing but no production order is running. It clears when an order starts. |
| **Machine off, order still running** | The machine has been off for the configured delay while an order keeps counting time. It clears when the order ends or the machine restarts. |
| **Order running too long** | One order has run longer than the configured threshold. |
| **Order carried across shift change** | An order from an earlier shift is still open after the current shift's delay. This needs shifts assigned to the machine. |
| **Live data is stale** | No fresh machine data has arrived for the configured delay. Other warnings may be unreliable. |
| **Multiple orders running** | More orders are running than **Jobs in parallel** allows for this machine. |
| **Downtime without a reason** | A downtime block is waiting for a reason. |

**Info** is a silent notice. **Warning** shows an orange banner and orange light around the whole page; **Critical** shows a red banner and flashing screen border. The warning appears after the delay selected by your administrator. A running-too-long warning uses its hour threshold instead of a separate delay.

Use the floating warning button at the bottom left: select **View all warnings** to open **Active warnings**. Read the affected order and duration, then use the available action: **Select an order**, **End order**, **Set downtime reason**, or **Refresh now**. Check the actual machine and order before acting. Follow [Run Data Acquisition](/en/platform-intro/workflows/data-acquisition).

### Snooze and Sound

You can snooze one warning or choose **Snooze all**. Snoozing hides the warning from the banner and silences it for the configured duration. It remains listed under **Snoozed** and returns if the problem persists. **Resume now** brings it back sooner.

If sound is allowed and enabled for that warning, **Warning** plays two beeps and **Critical** three alternating high and low beeps. The alarm repeats while the warning is active and not snoozed. **Info** is always silent. Select **Enable sound** to turn sound on or **Mute** to silence it on your device. The browser needs a tap on the page before sound can start.

Your administrator can limit sound to active shifts. This limits sound, not the warning display. See [Machine Configuration](/en/platform-intro/pages/machine-configuration) for delays, severity, sound, and copying settings.

## Outcomes and Limits

### Common Outcomes
- The machine’s production story is captured in a detailed, shift-level form.
- Recorded totals include the submitted scrap quantities.

### Limits and Empty States
- Users need the right live assignment or order context before the page is fully useful.
- The page warns users before production starts when another assignment is already running.
